# Task 05 – Galactic Year

## Problem Statement

Our Solar System orbits the center of the Milky Way (a distance of about 27,000 light-years) at a speed of about 220 km/s. How long does it take to complete one orbit (a "galactic year")?

## Theory

Assuming a circular orbit, the time required to complete one full revolution (orbital period $T$) can be derived from the circumference of the orbit and the constant orbital speed:

$$
T = \frac{C}{v} = \frac{2 \pi r}{v}
$$

Where:
* $r$ is the radius of the orbit.
* $v$ is the orbital velocity.

Units must be consistent before the final division.

## Step-by-Step Solution

1. Identify the given parameters:

$$
r = 27,000 \text{ ly}
$$

$$
v = 220 \text{ km/s}
$$

2. Convert the orbital radius from light-years to kilometers. One light-year is the distance light travels in one Julian year:

$$
1 \text{ ly} \approx 9.461 \times 10^{12} \text{ km}
$$

$$
r = 27,000 \times 9.461 \times 10^{12} \text{ km} \approx 2.554 \times 10^{17} \text{ km}
$$

3. Calculate the orbital circumference $C$:

$$
C = 2 \pi (2.554 \times 10^{17} \text{ km}) \approx 1.605 \times 10^{18} \text{ km}
$$

4. Calculate the orbital period $T$ in seconds:

$$
T = \frac{1.605 \times 10^{18} \text{ km}}{220 \text{ km/s}} \approx 7.295 \times 10^{15} \text{ s}
$$

5. Convert the period from seconds to years. One year contains approximately $3.154 \times 10^7$ seconds:

$$
T \text{ (years)} = \frac{7.295 \times 10^{15} \text{ s}}{3.154 \times 10^7 \text{ s/year}} \approx 2.31 \times 10^8 \text{ years}
$$

## Final Result

One galactic year corresponds to $2.31 \times 10^8 $ terrestrial years.

## Interpretation

The solar system has completed roughly 20 galactic orbits since its formation $4.6$ billion years ago. This long timescale highlights the massive physical scale of the Milky Way galaxy.
