# Task 06 – Galactic Collision

## Problem Statement

The Andromeda galaxy is about 2.5 million light-years away and is moving towards our Milky Way at about 110 km/s. Estimate how long it will be until the two galaxies collide.

## Theory

The time until collision can be estimated using basic kinematics, assuming the relative velocity between the two galaxies remains constant. The time $t$ is calculated by dividing the separation distance $d$ by the approach velocity $v$:

$$
t = \frac{d}{v}
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

1. Identify given variables:

$$
d = 2.5 \times 10^6 \text{ ly}
$$

$$
v = 110 \text{ km/s}
$$

2. Convert the distance into kilometers to match the velocity units:

$$
1 \text{ ly} \approx 9.461 \times 10^{12} \text{ km}
$$

$$
d = 2.5 \times 10^6 \times 9.461 \times 10^{12} \text{ km} \approx 2.365 \times 10^{19} \text{ km}
$$

3. Calculate the time in seconds:

$$
t = \frac{2.365 \times 10^{19} \text{ km}}{110 \text{ km/s}} \approx 2.15 \times 10^{17} \text{ s}
$$



## Final Result

Assuming constant velocity, the time for the collision will be  $2.15 \times 10^{17} \text{ s}$

## Interpretation

This is a first-order estimate. In reality, as the galaxies draw closer, mutual gravitational attraction will accelerate them, reducing the actual time until collision to closer to 4 to 5 billion years.
