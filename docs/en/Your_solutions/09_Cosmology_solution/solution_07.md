# Task 07 – Galactic Communication

## Problem Statement

How much time would it take to exchange mutual greetings between two civilizations located at opposite ends of the Milky Way, assuming they can communicate at the speed of light?

## Theory

By definition, light travels one light-year of distance in one year of time. Exchanging a mutual greeting requires a signal to travel across the diameter of the galaxy and a reply signal to travel the exact same distance back.

$$
t_{total} = t_{send} + t_{reply}
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

1. Identify the diameter of the Milky Way galaxy. The visible disk is generally estimated to be:

$$
D \approx 100,000 \text{ ly}
$$

2. Calculate the time for the initial message to reach the destination:

$$
t_{send} = \frac{D}{c} = 100,000 \text{ years}
$$

3. Calculate the time for the reply message to return:

$$
t_{reply} = \frac{D}{c} = 100,000 \text{ years}
$$

4. Compute the total exchange time:

$$
t_{total} = 100,000 + 100,000 = 200,000 \text{ years}
$$

## Final Result

It would take approximately 200,000 years to exchange mutual greetings.

## Interpretation

The vast transit times for electromagnetic signals pose a significant obstacle to any potential two-way communication with extraterrestrial intelligence across galactic scales.
