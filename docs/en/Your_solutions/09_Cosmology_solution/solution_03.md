# Task 03 – Cosmic Sizes

## Problem Statement

Order the following objects in the universe from smallest to largest: Earth, Milky Way galaxy, Solar System, observable universe, Sun, Andromeda galaxy, human, Moon, Pluto, Jupiter, Ceres, Local Group of galaxies.

## Theory

To establish an accurate ordering, characteristic diameters or lengths for each entity must be evaluated:

* **Human:** $\sim 1.7$ m
* **Ceres:** $\sim 940$ km
* **Pluto:** $\sim 2,370$ km
* **Moon:** $\sim 3,474$ km
* **Earth:** $\sim 12,742$ km
* **Jupiter:** $\sim 139,820$ km
* **Sun:** $\sim 1.39 \times 10^6$ km
* **Solar System:** $\sim 287$ billion km (to the Oort cloud boundary) or $\sim 10^{13}$ km
* **Milky Way galaxy:** $\sim 100,000$ light-years
* **Andromeda galaxy:** $\sim 220,000$ light-years
* **Local Group of galaxies:** $\sim 10$ million light-years
* **Observable universe:** $\sim 93$ billion light-years

## Step-by-Step Solution

Sort the entities based on their characteristic scales from smallest to largest.

1. Human (meters)
2. Dwarf planets and moons (hundreds to thousands of kilometers)
3. Rocky planets (tens of thousands of kilometers)
4. Gas giants (hundreds of thousands of kilometers)
5. Stars (millions of kilometers)
6. Planetary systems (light-years)
7. Galaxies (hundreds of thousands of light-years)
8. Galaxy clusters (millions of light-years)
9. The Universe (billions of light-years)

## Final Result

1. Human
2. Ceres
3. Pluto
4. Moon
5. Earth
6. Jupiter
7. Sun
8. Solar System
9. Milky Way galaxy
10. Andromeda galaxy
11. Local Group of galaxies
12. Observable universe

## Interpretation

The hierarchy demonstrates structural grouping in astrophysics, where smaller celestial bodies compose planetary systems, which in turn compose galaxies, leading up to large-scale universal structures.
