# Task 02 – Cosmic Temperatures

## Problem Statement

Order temperatures of objects in the universe from hottest to coldest: the core of the Sun, the surface of the Sun, the present Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation, interstellar space, temperature of the Universe at the nucleosynthesis era, human body temperature, temperature of the Universe at the recombination era, temperature of a black hole with 10 solar masses, ionization temperature of hydrogen.

## Theory

Different physical processes govern the temperatures of cosmic and macroscopic phenomena. Representative values must be established for each entity:

* **Universe at nucleosynthesis:** Requires energies capable of nuclear fusion, approximately $10^9$ K.
* **Core of the Sun:** Main sequence hydrogen fusion requires approximately $1.5 \times 10^7$ K.
* **Ionization temperature of hydrogen:** The characteristic thermal energy needed to ionize hydrogen gas is around $10^4$ K.
* **Surface of the Sun:** The photosphere emits as a blackbody at approximately $5,800$ K.
* **Universe at recombination era:** Neutral atoms form when the universe cools below the ionization threshold, occurring at roughly $3,000$ K.
* **Human body temperature:** Standard physiological temperature is $310$ K.
* **Interstellar space:** Dependent on proximity to stars, but cold molecular clouds sit around $10$ K to $50$ K.
* **Present CMB radiation:** The residual heat of the Big Bang, currently $2.7$ K.
* **Black hole (10 solar masses):** Hawking radiation temperature is inversely proportional to mass, resulting in approximately $6 \times 10^{-8}$ K.

## Step-by-Step Solution

Sort the representative temperatures in descending order:

1. $10^9$ K (Nucleosynthesis)
2. $1.5 \times 10^7$ K (Sun's core)
3. $10^4$ K (Hydrogen ionization)
4. $5,800$ K (Sun's surface)
5. $3,000$ K (Recombination)
6. $310$ K (Human body)
7. $\sim 20$ K (Interstellar space)
8. $2.7$ K (CMB)
9. $10^{-8}$ K (Black hole)

## Final Result

1. Temperature of the Universe at the nucleosynthesis era
2. Core of the Sun
3. Ionization temperature of hydrogen
4. Surface of the Sun
5. Temperature of the Universe at the recombination era
6. Human body temperature
7. Interstellar space
8. Present Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation
9. Temperature of a black hole with 10 solar masses

## Interpretation

The scale spans over 17 orders of magnitude, illustrating the extreme thermal gradients present in astrophysics, from early primordial epochs to the theoretical thermodynamic limits of black holes.
