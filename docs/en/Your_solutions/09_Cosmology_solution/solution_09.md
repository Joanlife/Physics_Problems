# Task 09 – Cosmic Expansion

## Problem Statement

If the universe is expanding at a rate of 70 (km/s)/Mpc, how much will the distance between two galaxies that are currently 100 million light-years apart increase after 1 billion years? Assume that the expansion rate remains constant over this time period. ($1 \text{ Mpc} \approx 3.26 \text{ million light-years}$).

## Theory

The change in distance ($\Delta d$) over a time period ($\Delta t$) is driven by the constant recessional velocity ($v$) of the galaxies relative to each other.

1. Find the recessional velocity using Hubble's Law:

$$
v = H_0 d
$$

2. Calculate the increased distance using basic kinematics:

$$
\Delta d = v \Delta t
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

1. Convert the current distance $d$ from million light-years (Mly) to megaparsecs (Mpc):

$$
d \text{ (Mpc)} = \frac{100 \text{ Mly}}{3.26 \text{ Mly/Mpc}} \approx 30.67 \text{ Mpc}
$$

2. Calculate the recessional velocity $v$:

$$
v = 70 \text{ (km/s)/Mpc} \times 30.67 \text{ Mpc} = 2,146.9 \text{ km/s}
$$

3. Convert the elapsed time $\Delta t$ into seconds:

$$
\Delta t = 10^9 \text{ years} \times 3.154 \times 10^7 \text{ s/year} = 3.154 \times 10^{16} \text{ s}
$$

4. Calculate the change in distance $\Delta d$ in kilometers:

$$
\Delta d = 2,146.9 \text{ km/s} \times 3.154 \times 10^{16} \text{ s} \approx 6.77 \times 10^{19} \text{ km}
$$

5. Convert the resulting distance increase back to light-years:

$$
\Delta d \text{ (ly)} = \frac{6.77 \times 10^{19} \text{ km}}{9.461 \times 10^{12} \text{ km/ly}} \approx 7.15 \times 10^6 \text{ ly}
$$

## Final Result

The distance between the two galaxies will increase by approximately 7.15 million light-years.

## Interpretation

Despite the vast velocity of over 2,100 km/s, the vastness of cosmic scales means that over a billion years, the proportional increase in distance is only about 7% of their original separation.
