# Task 01 – Cosmic Chronology

## Problem Statement

Place the following events of the early universe in chronological order (from earliest to latest): Recombination (formation of neutral atoms), Big Bang Nucleosynthesis, Inflation, Formation of first galaxies, Quark-gluon plasma era.

## Theory

The standard cosmological model outlines the evolution of the universe from a high-density, high-temperature state to its current structure. The timeline is defined by phase transitions and cooling, which allowed progressively more complex structures to form. 

1. **Inflation:** An epoch of exponential expansion occurring fractions of a second after the Big Bang.
2. **Quark-Gluon Plasma Era:** The universe was filled with a high-temperature plasma of fundamental particles before quarks combined into hadrons.
3. **Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN):** Protons and neutrons fused to form the first light atomic nuclei (mostly helium and lithium).
4. **Recombination:** The universe cooled sufficiently for electrons to bind with nuclei, forming neutral atoms and releasing the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) radiation.
5. **Formation of First Galaxies:** Gravitational collapse of dark matter and gas led to the first stars and galactic structures.

## Step-by-Step Solution

1. Identify the earliest event: Inflation establishes the macroscopic structure of the universe immediately post-Big Bang.
2. Order the particle formation eras: Quarks must exist before they bind into nucleons, and nucleons must exist before they bind into atomic nuclei (Nucleosynthesis).
3. Order the atomic and macroscopic eras: Nuclei must capture electrons (Recombination) before large masses of neutral gas can collapse into galaxies.

## Final Result

1. Inflation
2. Quark-gluon plasma era
3. Big Bang Nucleosynthesis
4. Recombination (formation of neutral atoms)
5. Formation of first galaxies

## Interpretation

The chronology reflects the cooling of the universe. High-energy fundamental interactions dominated the early stages, giving way to nuclear physics, then atomic physics, and finally astrophysics and gravitation as the temperature decreased.
